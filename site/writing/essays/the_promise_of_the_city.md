# The Promise of the City
## A Manifesto for Human Flourishing

*For I Dream of Thought — 6 November 2026*

## The Promise

A city is a promise.

The abundance of the surrounding countryside makes it possible for not everyone to farm. From that abundance emerge artisans, musicians, philosophers, teachers, engineers, scientists, storytellers and dreamers. A city becomes a place where people can develop gifts that might otherwise have remained undiscovered.

A city says:

> Bring your gifts.
>
> We will make space for them.

Education makes the same promise.

It exists not to make identical people, but to help individuals discover who they might become and how they might contribute to the wider human story.

At its best, education cultivates flourishing.

At its worst, it manages compliance.

## We Are Not Libraries of Facts

For too long, education has confused knowledge with wisdom.

We are not libraries of facts.

We are not repositories of information.

We are meaning-making creatures.

We love.  
We wonder.  
We question.  
We grieve.  
We create.  
We imagine.

A library can store information. A human being can transform it into understanding.

The purpose of education is not simply the accumulation of facts. It is the formation of human beings capable of making sense of the world, of one another, and of their place within it.

## Humanity Before Curriculum

Every child encounters humanity before they encounter the curriculum.

Before mathematics.  
Before history.  
Before grammar.

A child learns:

- Whether they are valued.
- Whether curiosity is welcomed.
- Whether mistakes are safe.
- Whether they belong.
- Whether learning is joyful or humiliating.
- Whether adults can be trusted.

Only then come algebra and irregular verbs.

Humanity comes first. The curriculum follows.

When we reverse that order, we risk losing sight of the learner.

## Connection, Construction, Conversion

My educational philosophy rests on three pillars.

### Connection

Learning begins with encounter.

A child encounters a story, an unfamiliar custom, a new language, a scientific idea or a different culture.

Connection is the moment curiosity awakens. It is the spark behind the question:

> Why?

Without connection, learning can become an empty exercise: information received, repeated and soon forgotten.

### Construction

Knowledge is not simply transferred.

It is built.

The learner constructs understanding through exploration, experimentation, reflection and dialogue.

Facts become ideas.  
Ideas become understanding.  
Understanding becomes perspective.

The teacher's task is not to fill an empty vessel, but to create the conditions in which understanding can take shape.

### Conversion

The deepest learning changes the learner.

Not merely:

> I learned something.

But:

> I see something differently. I can do something I could not do before. I have become someone a little different.

A student discovers they can speak English. A child discovers a talent they did not know they possessed. An adult encounters a worldview that challenges their assumptions.

Learning becomes transformation.

Connection.  
Construction.  
Conversion.

This is how human beings grow.

## Meaning Before Method

As a child in a Kent garden, I spent hours shaping flints from chalk.

Nobody taught me. Nobody assessed me. Nobody set learning objectives.

Yet I learned.

Why?

Because it mattered. There was a purpose. There was imagination. There was meaning.

The same principle followed me into teaching.

Students would ask:

> "Teach me the past tense."

I would begin with:

> "Yesterday I went to the market..."

Then I would ask them to build a story.

Language emerged from meaning. Grammar emerged from communication. Learning emerged from purpose.

Human beings do not learn simply because they are taught. They learn when something catches their attention, answers a need, stirs their imagination or matters to them.

## The Role of Belief

Many students do not struggle because they lack ability. They struggle because they have come to believe that ability is beyond them.

I carried a crystal star inscribed with a single word:

**BELIEF**

Sometimes my job was not to teach English. It was to help students believe they could learn English.

For a while, they borrowed my belief until they found their own.

Education is not only the sharing of knowledge. It is the cultivation of possibility.

## Curiosity and Civilisation

Civilisation depends upon curiosity, not certainty.

Certainty can close doors. Curiosity opens them.

A child sees an unfamiliar custom and asks:

> Why?

A scientist asks:

> Is this true?

A citizen asks:

> What evidence supports this?

A wise person asks:

> What might I be missing?

The health of a civilisation depends not only on what it knows, but on whether it continues to question, investigate and revise what it thinks it knows.

## Encountering Difference

A rounded education should expose us to different ways of seeing the world.

What does a Muslim believe?  
What does a Jew believe?  
What does a Hindu believe?  
What does a Catholic believe?  
What do I believe?

The goal is neither blind acceptance nor reflexive rejection.

The goal is understanding.

Through encounter, we enlarge our map of reality. We may still disagree, but our disagreement can be informed by knowledge rather than fear.

Without encounter, we risk becoming trapped within ourselves.

## The Tragedy of Humanity

The tragedy of humanity is not that we know too little.

It is that we so often become less than we might have been.

We reward credentials and neglect wisdom.  
We measure performance and neglect growth.  
We cultivate compliance where we should cultivate curiosity.  
We prepare people for examinations and then wonder why they struggle with life.

Every child arrives carrying possibilities.

Every society chooses whether to nurture or diminish them.

## AI and the Future of Education

Artificial intelligence can help someone learn Dutch. It can correct pronunciation, explain grammar, generate exercises and provide instant feedback.

These are useful things.

But the central question of education has never been merely how to deliver information. Nor can technology decide, on our behalf, what makes learning worthwhile or what kind of people we hope to become.

The question is no longer simply:

> How do we deliver information?

It is:

> What is worth learning?

Knowledge is becoming abundant.

Meaning remains scarce.

The future of education lies not in competing with machines, but in helping human beings become more fully human.

## The Promise We Must Defend

At its best, a school is a place of possibility.

At its worst, it becomes a holding pen where aspiration fades, curiosity is mocked and hope disappears.

The difference is not technology.

The difference is vision.

To all who seek to diminish this promise through dogma, manipulation, oppression, fear or the narrowing of the human spirit, I offer a promise in return:

> I will not stop.
>
> I will not stop encouraging curiosity where others demand conformity.
>
> I will not stop defending possibility where others see limitation.
>
> I will not stop asking questions where certainty masquerades as wisdom.
>
> I will not stop helping people discover who they might become.
>
> I will not stop believing that education exists for human flourishing.

Because the purpose of education is not merely to create skilled workers.

It is to help create thoughtful citizens.

Compassionate neighbours.

Curious minds.

Wise elders.

And fully human beings.

## Final Thought

Technology expands what we can do.

Education should expand who we are.

The promise of the city.  
The promise of civilisation.  
The promise of education.

They are all ultimately the same promise:

> Bring your gifts.
>
> Bring your questions.
>
> Bring your curiosity.
>
> We will help you become more fully yourself.
