Birds’ Nests

A place for eggs, danger, display—and ideas waiting to be born.

22 September 2026 — Richard Jenkins · Birds · Nature · The Writing Tree

Birds do not usually live in their nests all year round. With a few exceptions, a nest is a place created for breeding: somewhere to lay eggs and raise young. Yet some nests are used by the same pair year after year, while others are repaired, enlarged, or occupied by later generations of the same species.

A safe place to lay eggs: an intricately crafted, weed-lined cup, soft as the dawn, warm as a nuzzled muzzle beneath the blankets of life.

Some birds spend extraordinary time and effort creating structures simply to attract a mate. A male bowerbird builds and decorates an elaborate bower—not a nest, but a courtship stage. If a female is impressed, she later builds the practical nest in which she lays her eggs.

The idea behind the Writing Tree was to include nests as places where ideas could be born: somewhere to gather fragments, advertise new work, shelter an unfinished thought, or simply add decoration among the branches.

Nests are never entirely safe. They can be predated or parasitised. A female cuckoo lays her egg in another bird’s nest, leaving the host parents to raise it. In several cuckoo species, the newly hatched chick ejects the host’s eggs or young from the nest, claiming all the food and attention for itself.

Snakes, cats, lizards, corvids, and many other animals take eggs and chicks. Human beings have collected them too. I once watched a huge crow steal a pigeon’s egg, much to the noisy disgust of the pigeon.

Birds are not the only nest builders, and not all nests are cups woven from grass. Edible-nest swiftlets build with strands of hardened saliva. Some fish hollow out, guard, or decorate nests underwater. Turtles scrape chambers into sand. Crocodilians mound vegetation. Dinosaurs nested long before birds inherited the sky.

A nest may be no more than a scrape in the ground, or it may be an elaborate woven sculpture. It can be nursery, signal, shelter, trap, inheritance, or temporary theatre.

I hope you enjoy exploring the ideas, thoughts, and notes gathered in these nests.
