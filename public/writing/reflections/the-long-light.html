Four Reflections · Love

The Long Light

for an enduring partnership

We were not the fire that burns a room down fast, all light and no furniture left standing — we were the fire kept low through a long night, fed a little at a time, tended by hands that had learned, by then, exactly how much it needed.

Nobody warns you love gets quieter. That the hands find each other in sleep before either of you has decided to reach. That an argument, twenty years in, is not the crack in something — it's two people who trust the thing enough to bang on it and expect it to hold.

I used to think forever was a promise made once, loud, at an altar, and then simply true forever after. Now I know it's a promise remade in a thousand unremarkable rooms — over dishes, over illness, over the ordinary Tuesday when nothing happened except that we stayed.

This is what nobody tells you about enduring love: it isn't the absence of doubt. It's choosing, again, on the day the doubt arrives, the way you'd choose a house you already live in — not because it's perfect, but because you know where everything is, and where everything is, is home.

So let the young loves have their fireworks. Let them have the falling. I'll take the long light of a fire well tended, the two chairs worn to the shape of us, and the small, unspectacular miracle of another ordinary morning, chosen, with you still here.
