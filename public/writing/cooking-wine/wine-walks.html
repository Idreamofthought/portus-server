Wine Walks

2022-09-28

For the last two years I've participated in annual events where a group of people can walk around the vineyars and sample the produce of local wine makers. Yesterday I was lucky eniugh to participate in the Muscadet walk, in the small village of Saint Fiacre , near the "Capital" of Muscadet, Vallet, in France.

Muscadet is a dry white wine associated with seafood, fish and as a drink that could be mixed with sweeter syrups (either alcoholic or not) and made into a cocktail called 'Kir'

About Muscadet: It's had a bad press, being seen as a low quality Muscadet and most folks don't understand that there are many different types of Muscadet wines. They are all made from the "Melon de Bourgogne "grape variety, BUT the terroir and the slopes all change what kind of muscadet you'll get, plus the vinification process.

It was a chilly Sunday morning as we set off, the first fingers of autumn draped over the fields, but soon the sun's warmth came and as we walked jumpers and coats were quickly shed. Until the first stop, people chatted away about their days, and their jobs and lives. We came to the first producer, and were welcomed with a wonderful band playing lively music . Jazz and some typical French songs. The first wine was an amazing muscadet that surprised and delighted. A nibble and on we went. The sun claimed up and a hot day burnet the clouds away. We hugged the edges of the fields.or the shade, drinking water to stave off any cramp. We tasted a different wine at every stop, after about 2 or 3 kilometres.
