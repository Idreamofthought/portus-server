# Codex

## Purpose
The Codex is the game’s structured repository of knowledge: fragments of history, local lore, cultural patterns, and interpreted truth. It is both a player utility and a story layer.

## Function
The codex should help the player understand:
- the world of Portus
- the way the settlement interacts with memory and dream
- the symbolism behind key systems and events
- hidden consequences of choices and disasters

## Structure
### World entries
Descriptions of geography, societies, rituals, myths, and forgotten structures.

### System entries
Entries explaining game mechanics in a world-appropriate way rather than a dry spreadsheet tone.

### Event entries
Records created after major crises, emotional moments, or player-driven changes.

### Fragmented truths
Entries that conflict with one another, leaving room for uncertainty and interpretation.

## Tone
The Codex should sound like a learned but imperfect archive: rich, poetic, and a little uncertain. It should not read like a standard manual.

## Player value
- Reward curiosity
- contextualize systems
- support worldbuilding continuity
- deepen the emotional meaning of each settlement decision

## Best practice
Avoid pure exposition dumps. Use codex content to deepen the player’s relationship with the city and its mythic logic.

## Example entry themes
- memory storms
- the old sea-rituals
- factions built around relic care
- generational conflict between old and new settlement systems
